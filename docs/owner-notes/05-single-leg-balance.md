# Voice note 5 (owner, 2026-10-24): Exercise 4, Single-leg balance (Stage 1 or the feet-to-hips link; standing). Raw capture, build at CH.

## Starting position (standing)
- Feet about shoulder-width apart. Notice where the leg tries to collapse inwards.
- Align ankles, knees and hips. Don't rotate too far in or out; find the central point and stand there.
- Notice the toes engaging to hold you up, then release.
- Notice and release the core, shoulders, jaw and other places.
- Control yourself into the position, then release and relax into it.

## Conducting the exercise
- Lean onto one foot. Most people lean over a long way. Some lean is natural on one leg.
- Keep the hips and ribs stacked: not controlled; loose, fluid, breathing, not holding.
- On one leg you'll bring in much more tension. The big toe grips more, which is natural and good. Then release the grip a little and **sway**. The body realigns: spine and head straighten.
- **Hold for 90 seconds if able**, with a slight bend in the knee (just not locked out).
- Over time you end up straight, not leaning: "almost directly as you should be".

## Levels
- **Basic:** just hold the starting stance (two feet). Supported options: use a door, pole or similar to help balance.
- **Normal / intermediate:** the one-leg hold above.
- **Advanced:** eyes closed; holding counterweights; up on the balls of the feet (heels off).
- **The concept is always the same:**
  - Added complexity makes the body control and compensate more.
  - Allow it at first, because it lets the right muscles fire.
  - Then release the overcompensation (the parts that shouldn't be working).
  - Then release even the good control and tension, to find the equilibrium point "where the reflex is in". As loose and fluid as possible.
- **Purpose:** teaching balance and coordination using "precipitatory awareness". Correct term: **proprioception**.

## Reps note (owner)
- The other exercises are about 8, 10 or 12 reps, "doesn't really matter". This one is a 90-second hold.
