# Voice note 8 (owner, 2026-10-24): Exercise 6, Single-leg RDL (Romanian deadlift; hip hinge). Stage: knees/hips link. Raw capture, build at CH.
- **Starting position:** the same as the single-leg balance. Spend a lot of time here on one leg.
  - Notice where you start to engage pressure: knee rolling in, toes over-gripping, hip dipping, spine turning.
  - Fully straight but released. You're at the reflex point, "ready to engage" (owner said "reflexology point"; use reflex or readiness wording, not reflexology).
- **Exercise:** a hip hinge on one leg. You'll notice where you really have to tense to do the movement.
- **Slow, quality movement is "where the juice is"**. It teaches the body to hip hinge.
- It's a simple exercise; the focus is the philosophy.
- Levels not stated. Claude to propose a regression and progression in line with the owner's pattern (e.g. a supported kickstand or wall-assisted hinge; then eyes closed or a light weight, with the same release concept). Flag these as Claude additions.

## Levels (owner follow-up; supersedes "levels not stated" above)
- **Regression:** for older people, a double-leg hip hinge. For people without the balance, use a pole or similar for support.
- **Advanced:**
  - add weight;
  - close the eyes;
  - tempo or time control on the movement;
  - put cones out and reach to grab them in different directions (multi-directional reaching).

## Core philosophy, stated explicitly (use across the course)
- **A stable hip hinge with almost no range of motion beats going all the way and compensating** (bending at the spine, using parts that aren't needed). This applies to every movement.
