## Exercise 1: Short foot exercise (Stage 1, feet)
- Barefoot. It's paramount to sit on a **hard chair**.
- **Starting position (seated):**
  - Don't brace. Get the spine and head straight and the rib cage aligned (top down or bottom up, it doesn't matter).
  - Rotate the hips in or out as needed. Knees over toes. Twist or shuffle the hips on the seat to position.
  - Ribs stacked, rib flare not up. Shoulders loose, hands resting on the knees, head straight.
  - Notice where you're bringing tension and release it all. Release tension in the breath.
  - Breathe 360° into the ribs, not deeply. Find the balance point.
  - Owner's idea: this engages the deeper spinal muscles ("deeper rectus muscles"). Needs checking: likely the deep stabilisers (multifidus, transversus abdominis, diaphragm).
- **The exercise:**
  - Bring the ball of the foot back towards the heel **without letting the toes curl**, which raises the arch.
  - You'll notice the leg muscles engage. Do a few at first and notice the body tensing up and forgetting to breathe.
  - Even with the arch as high as it goes, you can still release lots and keep the arch in the same position. We overcompensate and over-control.
  - Hold for 5, then release.
- **Beginner:** just do the movement, with no conscious awareness needed. Toes not down (not curled), just raise and lower.
- **Intermediate:** do it and release the force you're using everywhere else: knee joint, quadriceps, hips, pulling in other areas.
- **Advanced:**
  - Add the breath. A 2–5 second hold at the top, counting only the hold, then very slow down and very slow up.
  - Focus on the movement with no tension. It's very hard to breathe and do everything at once.
  - Owner's sense: it teaches the body to relax under more load. Terminology: dual-tasking, relaxing under load.
- **Very clear message:** don't rush to advanced. It's better to stay at beginner and progress into releasing tension than to rush and carry compensation, extra force, tension or pressure. Even in the right muscles, too much is too much.
- **Purpose:** training the reflexes and the "deep motor neurons" (terminology needed: motor control, reflexive stabilisation, motor learning).
