# Voice note 10 (owner, 2026-10-24): Exercise 7, Bulgarian split squat (Stage 3, knees). Raw capture, build at CH.
- **Starting position:**
  - Begin like the single-leg balance and RDL: explore it and find it stable.
  - Then place the back foot up behind you (on a sofa, bench or similar). Before moving at all, notice where you may need to adjust the front or back foot.
  - Expect lots of compensation in the **hip flexors**. Claude to research the common compensations and breathing points (e.g. front knee caving, pelvis tilting or rotating, rib flare, back-foot gripping).
  - Find the equilibrium; it gets more refined over time.
  - The toes engage a lot more here. Keep them loose, relaxed, fluid and adaptable. Trust the reflexes.
- **Exercise:** down, then up. Don't bend too far forward at the spine; trust the position. Go as far as you can while keeping the position, then up.
- **Beginner / regression:** a simple (two-leg) squat, still checking for overcompensation.
- **Advanced:** single-leg work: pistol squats, or half dips; on a step. The owner recommends **single-leg pistol squats on a step**.
- **Remember: position over movement.**

## Follow-up (owner)
- Common fault: too much weight in the front heel. Aim for a neutral spread between the ball of the foot and the heel, with the toes still able to engage. Even engage them lightly.
- **Applies to all exercises: "engage, but don't grip".** Know the support is there whenever you need it, and trust it to come when it's required.
- The toe and foot response usually triggers on the **push back up**. On the descent you may feel it too, but mainly trust the balance and the position, then re-engage what's necessary.
- "Most of this comes from the ground." Ties into the ground-up philosophy and the foot as the foundation.
