# Voice note 7 (owner, 2026-10-24): Exercise 5, Double-leg calf raises (Stage 2, Achilles and calves). Raw capture, build at CH.

## Starting position
- On a step: balls of the feet on the edge, heels hanging off, feet almost flat. Knees slightly bent.
- Grip the toes on the edge, just to remember they're there, then relax into it (not fully). Hold the position and balance. A banister may be needed.
- Explore the body first: release, and notice where you're overcompensating or controlling.

## Conducting the exercise
- Slow calf raise: down to the point, then up.
- Engage the toes at the top, but don't over-engage them. Stay nice and fluid and in tune with the body.

## Levels
- **Beginner / regression:** on the floor (not the stairs), or just raising. A banister to start.
- **Normal:** on the step as above.
- **Advanced:** eyes shut, added weight, single leg.

## Watch for
- Hips shifting, ribs flaring up, ankles rolling out.
- Owner's own example: during calf raises the rib cage wants to flare up and turn to the right. Keep it there, breathe, release the tension.

## Key messages (some apply to ALL exercises; put them in the introduction too)
- Full range isn't needed at first. We're building **strength in the position we've got**. True for all exercises.
- **Never force into a position that makes you compensate.** Note this in the introduction.
- A bit of shaking is good and normal.
- **Pain: stop if it goes above 2–3 out of 10.**
- Purpose: teaching the central nervous system. Terminology: neuromuscular control, motor learning; Claude to add.

## Tempo (owner follow-up)
- About 1 second up, a 1–2 second pause at the top, then slowly down (a slow **eccentric**, or lowering, phase).
- Similar to physio-style calf work, but the focus is the fundamental position rather than strictly counting 3 seconds down.
