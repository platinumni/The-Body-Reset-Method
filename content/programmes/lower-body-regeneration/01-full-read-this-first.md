Welcome to Lower Body Regeneration.

Before you try a single exercise, please read this guide all the way through, slowly and properly. **Set aside about 45 minutes for your first read**, somewhere quiet, ideally before your first session. Your first few rounds will be slower too, around 25-35 minutes, as you read along and find each position. Once you understand it, a full round takes about 15-20 minutes. Come back to this guide often. Each time you return, you'll notice something you missed, because you'll be reading it with a body that has started to understand.

The exercises matter. The thinking behind them matters more.

## Most of us were never taught how to move

Over years of sitting, rushing, training hard and pushing through, the body learns to protect itself. It grips through the feet, braces through the knees and tightens around the hips. It happens so gradually that most people never realise they're doing it.

That holding comes at a cost. When muscles are always working to keep you safe, they can't do the job they're there for. Certain positions stop being available to you, simple movements begin to feel like effort, and you never quite trust yourself in a balanced position. Training harder on top of that only makes the pattern stronger.

> Strength built on top of tension and compensation simply makes the compensation stronger.

## The method in one line

> Release the tension. Find the muscles that should be working. Then release even those, back to the level your reflexes need. Trust your reflexes.

Every exercise in this programme follows that same order. First you let go of the gripping, forcing and control you don't need. Then you find the right muscles. Then, once they're working, you soften even those, until you reach a point of balance where your body's own reflexes hold you. **Engage, but don't grip.**

Taoism has a beautiful name for this: **wu wei**, often translated as "effortless action". Doing exactly what is needed, and nothing more. It isn't collapse, and it isn't laziness. It's the body doing its job, without you getting in the way.

## Letting go, in the body and in life

Most of us live under control. At first it's the wrong kind: the gripping that comes from pressure, habit, fear and the need to hold everything together. For many people it takes a crisis, an injury or a body that finally says enough before they go looking for something better. And they find it: better habits, better choices, a quieter mind, a deeper sense of meaning. In the body, it's the right muscles finally doing the work.

That's real progress, and it matters. But look closely and you'll see it's still control. The right kind, held tightly. We swap one grip for another, and the body, the mind and the spirit stay braced.

The final step is the hardest, and the most freeing: to release even the good control, and trust. Trust your reflexes. Trust your body. Trust the process. Trust yourself. Not giving up, and not falling apart, but letting what you've learned run without standing guard over it.

This is wu wei. It's why every exercise ends in release, and why every programme ends by letting go of control.

## On and off: the real skill

Letting go doesn't mean living without control. A heavy squat needs control. So does a deadlift, a golf swing, a martial art, or lifting a child from the floor. The problem is that most people can't switch it off. Their control is permanently on, gripped and tight, long after the moment that needed it has passed.

So the journey has four steps:

1. **Find the wrong control, and switch it off.** The gripping and compensation your body has learned over the years.
2. **Learn the right control,** from the ground up: the correct muscles, in the correct order, doing the correct job.
3. **Release even that,** until movement becomes fluid and your reflexes carry you.
4. **Switch it on and off at will.** Engaged the moment you need it, released the moment you don't. On, off, on, off.

> The real skill isn't switching control off. It's finding the right control in a body that has learned the wrong patterns, and then being able to switch it on and off whenever you need it.

At first you'll switch it on and off consciously. With practice, even the switch becomes unconscious: your body engages for the lift and lets go after it, without you having to decide. That's the fourth stage of learning again, unconscious competence, applied to control itself.

That's what proper function looks like: a body that moves freely between control and release, and never gets stuck in either.

> **Once you understand it, a full round takes about 15-20 minutes.** The first few times are slower, and that's exactly as it should be.

## How every starting position works

You'll spend more time in the starting positions than anywhere else, and that's deliberate. They are where the real learning happens. Each one follows the same quiet sequence:

1. **Nudge gently into position.** There is an element of control here: you may need to shift, twist or guide yourself into alignment. But it should be soft and subtle, never forced.
2. **Release.** After every small adjustment, let go of the effort it took.
3. **Engage only what needs to be engaged.**
4. **Release again, and maintain.**

When you get it right, you'll feel it. Many people describe it as almost floating: upright, supported and light, without holding themselves there.

A word on "stacking". When the guide asks you to stack your ribs over your pelvis, or your head over your spine, it never means bracing into a rigid posture. It means a gentle nudge towards alignment, followed by a release.

## The four stages of learning

Every new skill passes through four stages, and this programme is designed to walk you through all of them:

1. **Unconsciously incompetent:** you don't know you're gripping. It simply feels normal.
2. **Consciously incompetent:** you begin to notice it. This can feel frustrating, but it is real progress.
3. **Consciously competent:** you can do it well, as long as you concentrate.
4. **Unconsciously competent:** your body does it by itself. This is the goal.

The early exercises live mostly in stages 2 and 3. The final exercise, the pogo hop, is split into two parts on purpose: part 1 is conscious, and in part 2 you let go and trust your body.

## Why we start at the feet

Your feet are the foundation of every step you take. When your arches and toes aren't doing their job, the ankles, knees and hips above them have to compensate. So the programme begins at the ground and works upwards:

1. **Feet and balance:** short foot, toe yoga, toe spreading and single-leg balance.
2. **Shins, Achilles and calves:** tibialis raises and calf raises.
3. **Hips and knees:** glute bridge, clamshells, single-leg Romanian deadlift and Bulgarian split squat.
4. **Integrate:** pogo hops, the culmination of the programme.

The first four exercises are mostly about recognising, releasing, reconnecting and retraining. Strength is being built throughout, but from stage 2 onwards it becomes more of a focus.

## The principles behind every exercise

- **Position over range of motion.** A stable, released position with almost no movement is worth far more than a big movement full of compensation. We build strength in the position you have, and the range follows.
- **Never force yourself into a position that makes you compensate.** If you have to twist, lean, grip or hold your breath to get there, you've gone too far. Come back to where you can stay released.
- **Engage, but don't grip.** Trust that the support is there whenever you need it, and that it will arrive when it's required.
- **A little shaking is normal.** It's your nervous system learning.
- **Respect discomfort.** A little is fine. Stop if anything rises above 2-3 out of 10.
- **Don't rush to the advanced versions.** It's far better to stay at beginner level and patiently release tension than to rush ahead carrying extra force and compensation. Even in the right muscles, too much is too much.

## What the advanced versions are really doing

Each exercise has easier and harder versions. The harder ones rarely add much physical effort. Instead, they layer awareness on top of a simple movement: the breath, a count, a hold, your eyes closed, your attention on several parts of the body at once.

Something remarkable happens when you do this. A movement that felt easy suddenly becomes complex. You may notice your breath catching or held, tension creeping back into your jaw, shoulders or hips, or the urge to over-control and rush. None of this is failure. It's exactly the information you're looking for.

When it happens, pause, release, and if you need to, step back a level. The aim is to keep your body in the place where it's learning: challenged, but never overloaded or forced. This kind of training is often called dual-task training, and it's how a simple, well-controlled movement becomes something your body can rely on while life is busy around it.

## The demonstration videos

Most exercises have a short demonstration video at the bottom of the page. You're welcome to watch it first, so you can picture the movement. But please remember it shows only what the movement looks like. **The real value is in the starting position and the guidance**, and no video can give you that.

## How much, and how often

Everything here is **advised**, not prescribed.

- **Advised: one round a day,** if you have the time. A round is one set of every exercise, in order.
- **You don't need to train every day.** Some people practise daily because they love it; others two or three times a week. Both are fine.
- **Advised reps: around 8-10.** Do fewer if you need to, and more if it feels good. As your control improves, you'll often find you need fewer.
- **A round takes about 25-35 minutes** while you're learning, and about 15-20 minutes once you know it.

Please don't see this as another system to follow. This programme exists to free you from rigid structure, not to hand you a new one. It gives you a baseline: a deep understanding of how your body moves, and how to move without forcing. From there, you decide what structure you want to build in, whether that's another of our programmes, your own training, or the sport you love.

This is not a race. You're learning to connect with yourself and to know your body a little better. Your pace will depend on your age, your history and your ability, and every pace is the right one. There's no shame here, no ego, no comparison and no competition. Just you, paying attention.

## How to use this programme

1. **Full guide:** read everything for the first week or two, especially the starting positions. Use the Next button to move through one page at a time.
2. **Listen:** once you know the ideas, press play and follow along as you practise.
3. **Key points:** when you're comfortable, use the short version.
4. **Exercise sheet:** eventually, the whole programme on one page, to print or keep on your phone.

This is education and movement coaching. If you have a medical condition or an injury, check with your doctor or physiotherapist before you begin.
