Welcome to Lower Body Regeneration. Before you try a single exercise, read this section all the way through, and come back to it more than once. The exercises only work properly when you understand the thinking behind them.

## Most of us were never taught how to move

Over years of sitting, rushing, training hard and pushing through, the body learns to protect itself. It grips through the feet, braces through the knees and tightens around the hips. It happens so gradually that most people never realise they're doing it.

That holding comes at a cost. When muscles are always working to keep you safe, they can't do the job they're there for. Certain positions stop being available to you, simple movements start to feel like effort, and you never quite trust yourself in a balanced position. Training harder on top of that only makes the pattern stronger.

> Strength built on top of tension and compensation simply makes the compensation stronger.

## The method in one line

> Release the tension. Find the muscles that should be working. Then release even those, back to the level your reflexes need. Trust your reflexes.

Every exercise in this programme follows that same order. First you let go of the gripping, forcing and control you don't need. Then you find the right muscles. Then, once they're working, you let go of the extra effort in those too, until you reach a balance point where your body's own reflexes hold you. Engage, but don't grip.

There's an old idea from Taoism called **wu wei**, often translated as "effortless action": doing exactly what's needed and nothing more. That's the feeling we're after. It isn't laziness, and it isn't collapse. It's the body doing its job without you getting in the way.

## The four stages of learning

Every new skill goes through four stages, and this programme is built to walk you through all of them:

1. **Unconsciously incompetent:** you don't know you're gripping. It just feels normal.
2. **Consciously incompetent:** you start to notice it. This can feel frustrating, but it's real progress.
3. **Consciously competent:** you can do it well, but only when you concentrate.
4. **Unconsciously competent:** your body does it by itself. This is the goal.

The early exercises live mostly in stages 2 and 3. The final exercise, the pogo hops, is split into two parts on purpose: part 1 is conscious, and part 2 is where you let go and trust your body.

## Why we start at the feet

Your feet are the foundation for every step you take. When your arches and toes aren't doing their job, the ankles, knees and hips above them have to compensate. So this programme starts at the ground and works upwards:

1. **Feet and balance:** short foot, toe yoga, toe spreading, single-leg balance.
2. **Shins, Achilles and calves:** tibialis raises, calf raises.
3. **Hips and knees:** glute bridge, clamshells, single-leg Romanian deadlift, Bulgarian split squat.
4. **Integrate:** pogo hops, the culmination of the programme.

The first four exercises are mostly about recognising, releasing, reconnecting and retraining. Strengthening is happening throughout, but from stage 2 onwards it becomes more of a focus.

## The rules that apply to every exercise

- **Position over range of motion.** A stable, released position with almost no movement is worth far more than a big movement full of compensation. We build strength in the position you've got, and the range comes later.
- **Never force yourself into a position that makes you compensate.** If you have to twist, lean, grip or hold your breath to get there, you've gone too far. Come back to where you can stay released.
- **Engage, but don't grip.** Know the support is there whenever you need it, and trust it to come when it's needed.
- **A little shaking is good and normal.** It's your nervous system learning.
- **Pain:** a little discomfort is fine, but stop if anything goes above 2–3 out of 10.
- **Don't rush to the advanced versions.** It's better to stay at beginner level and slowly release tension than to rush ahead carrying extra force and compensation. Even in the right muscles, too much is too much.

## How much and how often (all advised)

- **Advised: one round a day,** depending on the time you have. One round means one set of every exercise, in order.
- **You don't need to do it every day.** Some people do it daily because they love it; others do it once, twice or three times a week. All of it is fine.
- **Advised reps: about 8–10,** but it doesn't really matter. Do fewer if you need to, more if it feels good. You'll often find you naturally need fewer reps as your control improves.
- If you have more time, go round a second time. You can skip an exercise, or spend longer on the ones you find most useful.
- **How long a round takes:** about 25–35 minutes while you're learning, and about 15–20 minutes once you know it.

Every rep, set and time in this programme is **advised**, not a rule. There's structure here, but too much structure becomes too much control. Use it as a guide, and tune in to your body.

## How to use this programme

1. **Full guide:** read everything for the first week or two, especially the starting positions. Use the Next button to move through one page at a time.
2. **Listen:** once you know the ideas, press play and follow along as you practise.
3. **Key points:** when you're comfortable, use the short version.
4. **Exercise sheet:** eventually, the whole programme on one page. Print it or save it to your phone.

Each exercise has easier and harder versions. The demonstration videos show what the movement looks like, nothing more. The real work is in the starting position and the guidance.

This is education and movement coaching. If you have a medical condition or an injury, check with your doctor or physiotherapist before you start.
