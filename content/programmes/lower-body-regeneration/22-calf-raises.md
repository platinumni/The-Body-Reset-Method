## Starting position
- Stand on a step with the **balls of your feet on the edge** and your heels hanging off, feet almost flat. Hold a banister or wall if you need to.
- Knees slightly bent.
- Grip the edge with your toes, just to remind yourself they're there, then relax into it (not completely).
- Hold the position and find your balance. Before you move, explore: release, and notice where you're overcompensating or controlling.

## Conducting the exercise
- Let your heels lower to a comfortable point, then **rise up slowly.**
- Advised tempo: about **1 second up, a 1–2 second pause at the top, then slowly down.** The slow lowering is where a lot of the strength is built.
- Engage the toes at the top, but don't over-engage them. Stay fluid and in tune with your body.
- You don't need full range. A smaller movement in a good position is worth more.

## What to watch for
- **Hips shifting** backwards or to one side.
- **Ribs flaring up.** Many people find their rib cage wants to flare and even turn slightly to one side. Notice it, keep breathing, and release the tension there.
- **Ankles rolling out** onto the little toes.
- **Gripping the banister** hard. Use it to balance, not to pull.
- **Pain:** stop if it goes above 2–3 out of 10. A bit of shaking is good and normal.

## Easier and harder versions
### Beginner
Do them on the flat floor instead of a step, holding a banister or wall.

### Intermediate
On the step, as above.

### Advanced
Close your eyes. Or hold a weight. Or go single-leg. Whatever you add, the same rule applies: allow the extra control at first, then release what isn't needed.

## Key points
- Balls of the feet on the step, soft knees.
- Advised: 1 s up, 1–2 s pause, slowly down.
- Engage the toes, don't grip.
- Ribs down, keep breathing.
