## Starting position
- Stand on a step with the **balls of your feet on the edge** and your heels free, feet almost level. Hold a banister or wall if you need to.
- Knees softly bent.
- Grip the edge with your toes, just to remind yourself they're there, then relax into it (not completely).
- Find your balance and settle. Before you move, explore: release, and notice where you're overcompensating or controlling.

## Conducting the exercise
- Let your heels lower to a comfortable point, then **rise slowly.**
- Advised tempo: about **1 second up, a pause of 1 to 2 seconds at the top, then slowly down.** The slow lowering is where much of the strength is built.
- Engage the toes at the top, but don't over-engage them. Stay fluid and in tune with your body.
- You don't need full range. A smaller movement in a good position is worth more.

## What to watch for
- **The hips shifting** backwards or to one side.
- **The ribs flaring up.** Many people find the rib cage wants to lift and even turn slightly to one side. Notice it, keep breathing, and release the tension there.
- **The ankles rolling out** onto the little toes.
- **Gripping the banister** hard. Use it for balance, not to pull.
- **Pain:** stop if it rises above 2 to 3 out of 10. A little shaking is good and normal.

## Easier and harder versions
### Beginner
On the flat floor instead of a step, holding a banister or wall.

### Intermediate
On the step, as above.

### Advanced
Close your eyes. Or hold a weight. Or move to a single leg.

Whatever you add, expect the body to answer with more control: the ribs lift, the hips shift, the toes clench. Allow it at first, then release what isn't needed. You want to stay in the zone where your body is learning, never where it's simply forcing its way through.

## Key points
- Balls of the feet on the step, soft knees.
- Advised: 1 second up, a short pause, slowly down.
- Engage the toes, don't grip.
- Ribs soft, keep breathing.
