## Starting position
- Barefoot. Sit on a **hard chair**, towards the front, with both feet flat on the floor.
- **Don't brace.** Bring your spine and head up straight and your rib cage stacked over your pelvis. It doesn't matter whether you build it from the top down or the bottom up.
- Shuffle or twist your hips on the seat until your **knees are in line with your toes**. Rotate the hips in or out a little if you need to.
- Ribs stacked, not flared up at the front. Shoulders loose, hands resting on your knees, head straight.
- Now notice where you're bringing tension: jaw, shoulders, stomach, thighs, toes. Release it all.
- Release the breath too. Breathe gently 360°, into the front, sides and back of the ribs. Not deep breaths, just easy ones.
- Find the balance point, where you're upright without holding yourself up.

You may need to control yourself into this position at first. That's fine. Once you're there, release the control and see if you can stay.

## Conducting the exercise
- Without letting your toes curl, draw the **ball of your foot back towards your heel**. The foot gets shorter and the arch lifts.
- You'll notice your leg muscles start to engage. Do a few at first and simply notice: does your body tense up? Do you forget to breathe?
- Hold for about 5 seconds, then slowly release.
- Here's the key: even with your arch as high as it goes, you can still release a lot of effort and keep the arch in exactly the same place. Most of us over-control and overcompensate. Find out how little you need.

## What to watch for
- **Toes curling or clawing** to fake the arch. Keep them long and relaxed.
- **Thighs, knees and hips tightening,** or the knee pulling in.
- **Holding your breath** while you hold the arch.
- **Jaw and shoulders** creeping up.
- **Slumping** back into the chair as you concentrate.

## Easier and harder versions
### Beginner
Just do the movement: raise and lower the arch with the toes long. No need for any extra awareness yet.

### Intermediate
Do the movement, and release the force you're using everywhere else: knees, thighs, hips, and anything pulling elsewhere in the body.

### Advanced
Add the breath. Hold the top for 2–5 seconds, counting only the hold, then go very slowly down and very slowly up. Keep breathing throughout, without any extra tension. It's surprisingly hard to breathe, count and move at once, and that's exactly what teaches your body to stay relaxed under a greater load.

**Don't rush to advanced.** It's far better to stay at beginner and gradually release tension than to rush ahead carrying compensation.

## Key points
- Hard chair, sit tall, don't brace. Knees over toes.
- Ball of the foot back towards the heel. Toes stay long.
- Hold about 5 seconds, then release.
- Keep the arch, lose the effort.
