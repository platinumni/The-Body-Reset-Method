## Starting position
- Sit barefoot on a **hard chair**, towards the front edge, with both feet flat on the floor.
- **Don't brace.** Gently nudge your spine and head upright, and let your rib cage settle over your pelvis. Build it from the top down or the bottom up; either works. Then release the effort it took.
- Shuffle or rotate your hips softly on the seat until your **knees line up with your toes**. Turn the hips in or out a little if you need to, then let go.
- Ribs softly stacked, not flared at the front. Shoulders loose, hands resting on your knees, head balanced.
- Now notice where tension is creeping in: the jaw, shoulders, stomach, thighs, toes. Release it all.
- Release the breath too. Let it move gently through 360°, into the front, sides and back of the ribs. Not deep breaths; easy ones.
- Find the balance point, where you're upright without holding yourself up. When it's right, you'll feel light, almost floating.

- **Your toes stay long and completely loose throughout.** They shouldn't be gripping or pressing into the floor at all. Check them now, before you begin, and let them soften.

## Conducting the exercise
- Keeping your toes long and loose, gently draw the **ball of your foot back towards your heel**. The foot shortens and the arch rises. The toes don't curl, claw or press down; they simply rest.
- You'll feel the muscles of the foot and lower leg engage. For the first few, simply observe. Does your body tense up elsewhere? Do you forget to breathe?
- Hold for about 5 seconds, then slowly release.
- Here's the heart of it: even with your arch as high as it will go, you can release a remarkable amount of effort and keep the arch exactly where it is. Most of us over-control. Discover how little you need.

## What to watch for
- **Toes curling, clawing or gripping the floor** to fake the arch. This is the most common one. Keep them long and fully loose.
- **The thighs, knees or hips tightening,** or the knee drawing inwards.
- **Holding your breath** while you hold the arch.
- **The jaw and shoulders** creeping up.
- **Slumping** back into the chair as you concentrate.

## Easier and harder versions
### Beginner
Simply do the movement: raise and lower the arch, toes long and loose. No extra awareness is needed yet.

### Intermediate
Do the movement, and release the force you're using everywhere else: the knees, the thighs, the hips, and anything pulling elsewhere in the body.

### Advanced
Add the breath and a count. Hold the top for 2 to 5 seconds, counting only the hold, then move very slowly down and very slowly up, breathing all the while.

You'll find this is surprisingly demanding. Breathing, counting, holding and moving at once makes a tiny movement complex, and you may notice the breath catching, the toes wanting to grip, or tension sneaking into the jaw. That's exactly what you're training: your body learning to stay relaxed while the load on your attention rises. If it becomes a struggle, step back a level and release.

**Don't rush to advanced.** It's far better to stay at beginner and gradually release tension than to rush ahead carrying compensation.

## Key points
- Hard chair, sit tall, don't brace. Knees over toes.
- Toes long and fully loose. No gripping.
- Ball of the foot towards the heel. Arch lifts. Hold about 5 seconds.
- Keep the arch, lose the effort.
