## Starting position
- Stand barefoot with your feet about shoulder-width apart, near a wall or door frame in case you need it.
- Notice where your legs try to collapse inwards.
- Line up your **ankles, knees and hips.** Don't turn the feet too far in or out; find the central point and stand there.
- Notice your toes gripping to hold you up, then release them.
- Notice and release your stomach, shoulders, jaw and anywhere else you're holding.
- You may need to control yourself into the position. Then release, and relax into it.

## Conducting the exercise
- Lean onto one foot and lift the other. Most people lean a long way over at first. Some lean is natural on one leg.
- Keep your hips and ribs stacked, but not held. Loose, fluid and breathing.
- On one leg you'll bring in much more tension. Your big toe will grip more, which is natural and good. Then release that grip a little and let yourself **sway**. As you do, your body starts to realign itself, and your spine and head straighten.
- Keep a slight bend in the standing knee. Don't lock it out.
- **Hold for up to 90 seconds** if you can, then swap legs.
- Over time you'll find you stand straighter, and lean less.

## What to watch for
- **The knee rolling inwards.**
- **The hip dropping** or swinging out to the side.
- **Leaning a long way** over the standing leg.
- **Toes clawing** hard and staying gripped.
- **Holding the breath,** locking the knee, or stiffening the whole body to stop the sway.

## Easier and harder versions
### Beginner
Stay on two feet: just hold the starting stance, and practise releasing. Then try one leg while holding a door frame, pole or wall.

### Intermediate
The one-leg hold as described above.

### Advanced
Close your eyes. Or hold a light weight. Or rise onto the ball of the foot, heel off the floor.

**The concept is always the same.** Extra difficulty makes your body control and compensate more. Allow that at first, because it lets the right muscles switch on. Then release the overcompensation, the parts that shouldn't be working. Then release even the good control and tension, to find the balance point where your reflexes take over. As loose and fluid as possible.

## Key points
- Ankles, knees and hips in line.
- Lean onto one foot, soft knee.
- Let the toes grip, then release a little and sway.
- Advised: up to 90 seconds each leg.
