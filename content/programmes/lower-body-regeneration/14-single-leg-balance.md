## Starting position
- Stand barefoot with your feet about shoulder-width apart, near a wall or door frame in case you need it.
- Notice where your legs try to collapse inwards.
- Gently align your **ankles, knees and hips**. Don't turn the feet too far in or out; find the central point and stand there.
- Let the stacking continue all the way up: pelvis, ribs, spine and head, each balanced softly on the one below. A gentle nudge, then release.
- Notice your toes gripping to hold you up, then let them soften.
- Notice and release your stomach, shoulders, jaw and anywhere else you're holding.
- Guide yourself into the position, then release and relax into it.

## Conducting the exercise
- Shift onto one foot and lift the other. Most people lean a long way at first. Some lean is natural on one leg.
- Keep your hips and ribs stacked, but not held: loose, fluid and breathing.
- On one leg, much more tension arrives. Your toes, especially the big toe, will grip the floor. That's natural and good. It's the consciously competent stage: you're gripping, and you've got it.
- Now begin to release that grip. You still want it available the instant you need it, so it can engage only when it's necessary. As you sway left or right, you'll feel it engage too much. Let it, then release again.
- Let yourself **sway**. As you do, your body begins to realign itself: the spine lengthens and the head finds its place.
- Keep a slight bend in the standing knee. Don't lock it.
- **Hold for up to 90 seconds** if you can, then change legs.
- Over time you'll stand straighter and lean less, as your body settles naturally into its equilibrium point.

## What to watch for
- **The knee rolling inwards.**
- **The hip dropping** or swinging out to the side.
- **Leaning a long way** over the standing leg.
- **The toes clawing** hard and staying locked.
- **Holding the breath,** locking the knee, or stiffening the whole body to stop the sway.

## Easier and harder versions
### Beginner
Stay on two feet: hold the starting stance and practise releasing. Then try one leg while holding a door frame, pole or wall.

### Intermediate
The one-leg hold, as described above.

### Advanced
Close your eyes. Or hold a light weight. Or rise onto the ball of the foot, heel off the floor.

Each of these removes something your balance has been relying on, so your body will reach for control: the toes grip harder, the breath holds, the shoulders rise. Allow that at first, because it lets the right muscles switch on. Then release the overcompensation, the parts that shouldn't be working. Then release even the good control, to find the balance point where your reflexes take over. As loose and fluid as you can be.

## Key points
- Softly stacked from ankle to head.
- Lean onto one foot, soft knee.
- Let the toes grip, then release, and let yourself sway.
- Advised: up to 90 seconds on each leg.
