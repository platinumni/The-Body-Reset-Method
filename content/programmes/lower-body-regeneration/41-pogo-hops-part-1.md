## Starting position
- Stand on two feet, hip-width apart, with a soft bend in the knees.
- Release tension everywhere you can: breathe, soften the jaw, and relax the face and the muscles around the eyes.
- Grip the toes very lightly, so you **consciously know they're there.**

## Conducting the exercise
- Bounce gently on the balls of your feet.
- At first, your **heels should only just tap the floor.** That's all.
- Keep the knees soft and the rest of the body loose. Let the rhythm find you.
- Advised: anywhere from 20 seconds up to 5 minutes, depending on your fitness.

## What to watch for
- **Bouncing too high**, with the heels far off the floor and everything over-engaged. This is the most common fault.
- **Stiff, locked knees.**
- **Holding the breath,** or tension in the jaw, face and shoulders.
- **Landing heavily** instead of springing.

## Easier and harder versions
### Beginner
Rest your hands on the kitchen counter to help you push up. Or simply lift the heels a little, with a light toe grip, without leaving the floor.

### Intermediate
The pogo hop, as above.

### Advanced
A boxer skip, bouncing from one foot to the other with a transfer of weight. Or vary the height, building up to full, high bounces that use your whole body. Many people won't be able to do this, and that's perfectly fine. As the rhythm gets more complex, notice the breath and the jaw; they're usually the first to tighten.

## Key points
- Soft knees, heels just tapping.
- A light, conscious toe grip.
- Breathe; soft jaw and face.
- Advised: 20 seconds up to 5 minutes.
