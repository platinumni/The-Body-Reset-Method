## Starting position
- Lie on your back on a mat, knees bent, feet flat and hip-width apart, about a hand's length from your bottom.
- Feel the **three points of each foot** on the floor: the heel, the ball of the big toe and the ball of the little toe.
- Arms relaxed by your sides. Let your back rest in its natural curve; don't flatten or arch it.
- Release the jaw, shoulders, neck and stomach. Let the breath move through the ribs, back and sides.

## Conducting the exercise
- Press softly through the whole foot and **lift your hips** until your body forms a line from your shoulders to your knees. Rise only as high as you can without arching your back.
- Pause at the top and breathe.
- Lower slowly, one part of the spine at a time.
- Notice which muscles are lifting you. It should be mainly the glutes, not the lower back or the hamstrings.

## What to watch for
- **Arching the lower back** to gain height, with the ribs flaring.
- **The hamstrings cramping:** often the feet are too far away. Bring them a little closer.
- **The knees falling in** or pushing out.
- **Pushing up through the neck** and shoulders.
- **The toes gripping** or lifting.

## Easier and harder versions
### Beginner
A small lift: peel the hips a few centimetres off the floor and lower.

### Intermediate
The full bridge, as above, with a pause at the top.

### Advanced
A single-leg bridge, with the other knee drawn in or the leg held straight. Or a longer hold at the top, breathing slowly, or a resistance band just above the knees. Notice how quickly the back and neck try to take over as it gets harder, and keep handing the work back to the glutes.

## Key points
- Three points of each foot on the floor.
- Lift with the glutes, not the back.
- Pause, breathe, lower slowly.
