## Starting position
- Lie on your back on a mat, knees bent, feet flat on the floor and hip-width apart, about a hand's length from your bottom.
- Feel the **three points of each foot** on the floor: the heel, the ball of the big toe and the ball of the little toe.
- Arms relaxed by your sides. Let your back rest naturally; don't flatten or arch it.
- Release your jaw, shoulders, neck and stomach. Breathe 360° into the ribs, back and sides.

## Conducting the exercise
- Press gently through the whole foot and **lift your hips** until your body is in a line from your shoulders to your knees. Only go as high as you can without arching your back.
- Pause at the top and breathe.
- Lower slowly, one part of the spine at a time.
- Notice which muscles are lifting you. It should be mainly the glutes, not the lower back or the hamstrings.

## What to watch for
- **Arching the lower back** to get higher, with the ribs flaring.
- **Hamstrings cramping:** often the feet are too far away. Bring them a little closer.
- **Knees falling in** or pushing out.
- **Pushing up through the neck** and shoulders.
- **Toes gripping** or lifting.

## Easier and harder versions
### Beginner
A small lift: just peel the hips a few centimetres off the floor and lower.

### Intermediate
The full bridge, as above, with a pause at the top.

### Advanced
A single-leg bridge, with the other knee hugged in or the leg held straight. Or hold the top for longer, or add a resistance band just above the knees.

## Key points
- Three points of each foot on the floor.
- Lift with the glutes, not the back.
- Pause, breathe, lower slowly.
