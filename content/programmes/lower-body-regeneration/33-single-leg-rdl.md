## Starting position
- Begin exactly as you would for the **single-leg balance.** Spend real time here, on one leg.
- Notice where pressure starts to build: the knee rolling in, the toes over-gripping, the hip dipping, the spine turning.
- Find the position where you're tall but released, **ready to engage.** This is the point where your reflexes are poised to do their job.

## Conducting the exercise
- Keeping a soft bend in the standing knee, **hinge at the hip:** your chest travels forward and down as the free leg reaches back behind you.
- You'll notice exactly where you have to tense to make this happen. That's the information you're looking for.
- Go only as far as you can while staying stable and released, then return slowly.
- **Slow, quality movement is where the real value lies.** It teaches your body to hinge at the hip. The exercise is simple; the depth is in the philosophy.

## What to watch for
- **Bending at the spine** instead of the hip.
- **The hip of the free leg opening** and turning out to the side.
- **The standing knee** rolling in or locking straight.
- **The toes clawing** and staying gripped.
- **Rushing** to get lower. A small, stable hinge beats a deep, unsteady one.

## Easier and harder versions
### Beginner
A double-leg hip hinge: both feet on the floor, hands on your thighs, pushing the hips back. This is a good choice for older people too. If balance is the challenge, do the single-leg version holding a pole, chair or wall.

### Intermediate
The single-leg hinge, as above.

### Advanced
Add a weight. Or close your eyes. Or control the tempo, perhaps 3 seconds down and 3 seconds up. Or place a few cones (or any objects) around you and reach down to touch them in different directions.

Each of these adds a new demand on your attention, and you'll feel the old habits return: the spine rounding, the breath holding, the toes clenching. Meet each one with a release. You want to be learning, not wrestling.

## Key points
- Begin in your single-leg balance.
- Hinge at the hip, not the spine.
- Only as far as you stay stable.
- Slow is where the value lies.
