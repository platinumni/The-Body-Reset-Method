## Starting position
- Begin as you did for the single-leg balance and the hinge: stand, explore, and find a stable, released position.
- Then place the **back foot up behind you** on a sofa, bench or sturdy chair.
- Before you move at all, notice whether the front or back foot needs adjusting.
- Expect plenty of compensation through the **hip flexors** at the front of the back leg. Other common ones: the front knee caving in, the pelvis tilting or twisting, the ribs flaring, and the back foot gripping.
- Spread your weight evenly between the **ball of the front foot and its heel.** Too much weight in the heel is the most common fault. The toes should still be able to engage, even lightly.
- Find your balance point. It will grow more refined over time.

## Conducting the exercise
- Lower **down**, then rise back **up.**
- Don't lean far forward at the spine; trust the position.
- Go only as far as you can while holding the position, then rise.
- The toes engage a great deal more here. Keep them loose and adaptable. You'll usually feel the foot respond most on the **push back up.** On the way down, mainly trust your balance and the position, then re-engage what you need.
- Most of this comes from the ground.

> Remember: position over movement.

## What to watch for
- **Too much weight in the front heel.**
- **The front knee caving** inwards.
- **The pelvis twisting** or tipping forward, with tension building at the front of the back hip.
- **Leaning far forward** at the spine.
- **The back foot gripping** the sofa.
- **Holding the breath.**

## Easier and harder versions
### Beginner
A simple two-leg squat, still watching for all the same overcompensation.

### Intermediate
The Bulgarian split squat, as above.

### Advanced
Single-leg work on a step: half single-leg squats, or full **pistol squats on a step**, which is the one recommended here.

At this level the body will want to grip everything: toes, hips, jaw, breath. Work in the range where you can still release, even if that's only a few centimetres. That is where the learning happens.

## Key points
- Back foot up. Weight spread across the front foot.
- Lower only as far as you hold the position.
- The feet respond on the way up. Engage, don't grip.
- Position over movement.
