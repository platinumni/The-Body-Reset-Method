You've reached the end of the core programme, and this may be the most important page in it.

## You're meant to outgrow this

The aim was never to give you another routine to keep up for the rest of your life. There is a phase of teaching the body. For some people it takes weeks or months; others find it in a week or two. Either is fine. But once you genuinely have the control, you can let go of most of the structure.

The exercises in this programme are good. So are the exercises in a great many other programmes, and they'll bring good results. But what we've really been teaching you sits underneath all of them: the depth of the movement philosophy, and a living awareness of your own body. That is the core of it, and it stays with you.

## What's always worth keeping

When you want a short, simple practice, these are always time well spent:

- **Activate the arches.** A few short foot reps take less than a minute, and wake the whole foundation up.
- **Single-leg balance.** Always worth doing, anywhere.
- **A few calf raises**, and some **single-leg work**, such as split squats or the advanced single-leg bends, once you have control of them.
- **Pogo hops.** Always good.
- **A skipping rope.** Highly recommended: rhythmic, light and released, it's everything this programme is about.

A couple of minutes of this, done with real awareness, will do more than a long session on autopilot.

## If you'd like to keep building

Here are some simple bodyweight exercises to add. They come without the full guidance, because by now you know how to approach them: find the position, release what isn't needed, engage but don't grip, and keep position over range.

**Bodyweight**

- **Knees-over-toes lunges:** a long lunge where the front knee travels forward over the toes, heel staying down. Start small.
- **Split squats:** like the Bulgarian split squat, with the back foot on the floor.
- **Reverse lunges:** step back into a lunge, then return. Often kinder on the knees than stepping forward.
- **Step-ups:** onto a step or stair, slowly, with the front foot doing the work.
- **Single-leg calf raises** on a step.

**At home with a resistance band**

- **Banded glute bridges:** band just above the knees.
- **Banded side steps:** band around the ankles or just above the knees, small steps sideways.
- **Banded clamshells.**
- **Banded tibialis raises:** band around the front of the foot, anchored low in front of you.

## Taking it into your life, your training and your sport

- Before training or playing, a short warm-up of short foot, single-leg balance and pogo hops is plenty.
- In anything you do, return to the same three questions: What am I gripping or forcing? Which muscles should be working? Can I do this with less effort and still stay stable?
- When you add load or speed, expect old habits to return for a while. Allow it, then release what isn't needed, just as you've practised.
- The same concept lives in the way you walk, lift your children or grandchildren, swing a golf club or train a martial art. Everything starts at the feet.
