## Starting position
Almost exactly the same as the short foot. **Spend time here.** The better your starting position, the more you'll get from the exercise.

- Barefoot. Sit on a **hard chair**, towards the front, with both feet flat on the floor.
- **Don't brace.** Bring your spine and head up straight and your rib cage stacked over your pelvis. It doesn't matter whether you build it from the top down or the bottom up.
- Shuffle or twist your hips on the seat until your **knees are in line with your toes**. Rotate the hips in or out a little if you need to.
- Ribs stacked, not flared up at the front. Shoulders loose, hands resting on your knees, head straight.
- Now notice where you're bringing tension: jaw, shoulders, stomach, thighs, toes. Release it all.
- Release the breath too. Breathe gently 360°, into the front, sides and back of the ribs. Not deep breaths, just easy ones.
- Find the balance point, where you're upright without holding yourself up.

- You may need to force or control yourself into position a little at first. Once you're straight, release that area.
- Imagine your rib cage floating as you breathe, fully loose. You might even feel slightly "wavy". That's good.

## Conducting the exercise
- Press your **big toe** gently into the floor and lift the **other four toes** up. Hold for a couple of seconds, then lower them slowly.
- Then swap: the four toes stay down and the big toe lifts. Hold, and lower slowly.
- Keep going back and forth, slowly.

## What to watch for
- **Pressing the big toe far too hard.** Almost everyone does this at first.
- **Glutes tensing** and the hips gripping.
- **Ribs and spine twisting** to help the toes.
- **Forgetting to breathe.**
- The whole foot rolling in or out instead of just the toes moving.

## Easier and harder versions
### Beginner
Just do the movement, back and forth.

### Intermediate
Consciously notice where you can release tension. Then play with the pressure in the big toe: reduce it until you cross the point where it's barely touching the floor, then re-engage. Find the sweet spot, while still lifting the four toes as high as you can.

### Advanced
Count the holds while you keep breathing, without letting the counting fall into rhythm with your breath. Count each time the big toe is down, and the same for the four toes. When the other tension (glutes, hips, spine) has gone and it's just the toes working, release another 5–10%. Feel the point where the toes let go too much and want to come up. Find that beautiful spot just before it.

## Key points
- Same seated start: tall, released, breathing.
- Big toe down, four up. Then swap. Slowly.
- Less pressure than you think.
- Only the toes are working.
