## Starting position
Almost exactly the same as the short foot. **Give this time.** The better your starting position, the more this exercise will teach you.

- Sit barefoot on a **hard chair**, towards the front edge, with both feet flat on the floor.
- **Don't brace.** Gently nudge your spine and head upright, and let your rib cage settle over your pelvis. Build it from the top down or the bottom up; either works. Then release the effort it took.
- Shuffle or rotate your hips softly on the seat until your **knees line up with your toes**. Turn the hips in or out a little if you need to, then let go.
- Ribs softly stacked, not flared at the front. Shoulders loose, hands resting on your knees, head balanced.
- Now notice where tension is creeping in: the jaw, shoulders, stomach, thighs, toes. Release it all.
- Release the breath too. Let it move gently through 360°, into the front, sides and back of the ribs. Not deep breaths; easy ones.
- Find the balance point, where you're upright without holding yourself up. When it's right, you'll feel light, almost floating.

- You may need a little control to guide yourself into place at first. Once you're there, release that control and see whether you can stay.
- Imagine your rib cage floating as you breathe, fully loose. You might even feel slightly "wavy". That's a good sign.

## Conducting the exercise
- Press your **big toe** gently into the floor and lift the **other four toes** away from it. Hold for a couple of seconds, then lower them slowly.
- Then swap: the four toes rest down and the big toe lifts. Hold, and lower slowly.
- Continue back and forth, unhurried.

## What to watch for
- **Pressing the big toe far too hard.** Almost everyone does this at first.
- **The glutes tensing** and the hips gripping.
- **The ribs and spine twisting** to help the toes.
- **Forgetting to breathe.**
- **The whole foot rolling** in or out, instead of only the toes moving.

## Easier and harder versions
### Beginner
Simply do the movement, back and forth.

### Intermediate
Notice where you can release tension. Then explore the pressure in the big toe: ease it off until it's barely touching the floor, then re-engage. Find the sweet spot, while still lifting the four toes as high as you comfortably can.

### Advanced
Count the holds while you keep breathing, without letting the count fall into rhythm with your breath. Count each time the big toe is down, and the same for the four toes.

This splits your attention several ways at once, and you'll feel the difference: the breath wants to sync with the count, the pressure creeps up, the hips quietly join in. Notice each one and release it. When the other tension has gone and it's just the toes working, release another 5 to 10 percent. Feel the point where the toes let go too much and want to lift. Find that beautiful spot just before it.

## Key points
- Same seated start: tall, released, breathing.
- Big toe down, four up. Then swap. Slowly.
- Less pressure than you think.
- Only the toes are working.
