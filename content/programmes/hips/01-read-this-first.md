Welcome to the Hips programme. It's built on exactly the same philosophy as Lower Body Regeneration. If you've done that programme, you already know the method; read this as a reminder. If you're starting here, read it carefully, because the exercises only work properly when you understand the thinking behind them.

## The method in one line

> Release the tension. Find the muscles that should be working. Then release even those, back to the level your reflexes need. Trust your reflexes.

The hips are where a huge amount of gripping lives. Years of sitting, bracing and forcing leave them tight in some directions and weak in others, and the body learns to lean, twist and borrow from the lower back to get around it.

So this programme does two things, in this order:

1. **Open:** gentle openers and stretches to find the range you have, without forcing.
2. **Strengthen:** build control and strength in that range, so the body can trust it.

## The rules that apply to every exercise

- **Build range off the correct pattern.** A smaller movement done well beats a big one done with compensation. The range will come.
- **Never force into a position that makes you compensate.** No leaning, twisting or holding your breath to get further.
- **Engage, but don't grip.**
- **A little shaking is normal.** Stop if pain goes above 2–3 out of 10.
- **Don't rush to the advanced versions.**

## The four stages of learning

You'll move from not noticing your gripping (unconsciously incompetent), to noticing it (consciously incompetent), to doing it well when you concentrate (consciously competent), and finally to your body doing it by itself (unconsciously competent). That last one is the goal.

## How much and how often (all advised)

- **Advised: one round a day,** if you have the time. Not every day is needed.
- **Advised reps: about 8–10,** or the hold time shown.
- A round takes about 20 minutes while you're learning, and 10–15 once you know it.
- If you also have the Core programme, you can do both together, alternate them day by day, or split them across the week.

## What you'll need

A mat or towel, a wall or chair to hold, a step or stair, and a cushion. Optional for the harder versions: a resistance band and a light weight.

This is education and movement coaching. If you have a medical condition or an injury, check with your doctor or physiotherapist before you start.
