Welcome to the Hips programme.

Before your first session, please read the whole programme through, slowly and properly. **Allow about 20 minutes for your first read.** Then return to it often.

The Hips programme is built on exactly the same philosophy as Lower Body Regeneration. If you've done that programme, read this as a reminder. If you're starting here, take your time with it, because the exercises only work properly when you understand the thinking behind them.

## The method in one line

> Release the tension. Find the muscles that should be working. Then release even those, back to the level your reflexes need. Trust your reflexes.

The hips hold a remarkable amount of tension. Years of sitting, bracing and pushing through leave them tight in some directions and underused in others, and the body learns to lean, twist and borrow from the lower back to get around it.

So this programme works in two parts, in this order:

1. **Open:** gentle openers and stretches that find the range you already have, without forcing.
2. **Strengthen:** control and strength in that range, so your body can trust it.

## How every starting position works

1. **Nudge gently into position.** There's an element of control, but it's soft and subtle, never forced.
2. **Release** the effort it took.
3. **Engage only what needs to be engaged.**
4. **Release again, and maintain.**

When it's right, you'll feel it: supported, light, almost floating.

## The principles behind every exercise

- **Build range off the correct pattern.** A smaller movement done well beats a large one full of compensation. The range will come.
- **Never force into a position that makes you compensate.** No leaning, twisting or holding your breath to get further.
- **Engage, but don't grip.**
- **A little shaking is normal.** Stop if discomfort rises above 2 to 3 out of 10.
- **Don't rush to the advanced versions.**

## The four stages of learning

You'll move from not noticing your gripping (unconsciously incompetent), to noticing it (consciously incompetent), to doing it well when you concentrate (consciously competent), and finally to your body doing it by itself (unconsciously competent). That last one is the goal.

## How much, and how often

Everything here is **advised**, not prescribed.

- **Advised: one round a day,** if you have the time. Not every day is needed.
- **Advised reps: around 8 to 10,** or the breaths or hold time shown.
- A round takes about 20 minutes while you're learning, and 10 to 15 once you know it.
- If you have both Hips and Core, you can do them together, alternate them day by day, or spread them across the week.

Please don't see this as another system to follow. The aim is to free you from rigid structure, not to hand you a new one. This gives you a baseline, and from there you choose what to build: another of our programmes, your own training, or your sport.

This is not a race. You're learning to connect with yourself and know your body better, and your pace will depend on your age and ability. Every pace is the right one. No shame, no ego, no comparison.

## What the advanced versions are really doing

The harder versions rarely add much effort. They layer awareness onto a simple movement: the breath, a count, a hold, less support. A movement that felt easy suddenly becomes complex, and you may notice the breath holding, tension returning or the urge to rush. That's the information you're looking for. Pause, release, and step back a level if you need to. Stay where your body is learning, never where it's forcing.

## The demonstration videos

Most exercises have a short demonstration video at the bottom of the page. Watch it first if it helps you picture the movement, but remember it shows only what the movement looks like. **The real value is in the starting position and the guidance.**

## Where the full philosophy lives

This programme includes a summary of the philosophy, enough to practise it well. The complete teaching, with the reasoning behind every principle, is in our flagship programme, **Lower Body Regeneration**. If you haven't done it, we recommend starting there.

## What you'll need

A mat or towel, a wall or chair to hold, a step or stair, and a cushion. Optional for the harder versions: a resistance band and a light weight.

This is education and movement coaching. If you have a medical condition or an injury, check with your doctor or physiotherapist before you begin.
