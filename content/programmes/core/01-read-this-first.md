Welcome to the Core programme. It's built on exactly the same philosophy as Lower Body Regeneration. If you've done that programme, read this as a reminder. If you're starting here, read it carefully.

## The method in one line

> Release the tension. Find the muscles that should be working. Then release even those, back to the level your reflexes need. Trust your reflexes.

Most people think of the core as something you brace: suck in, squeeze, hold. This programme takes the opposite approach. A well-working core is mostly reflexive. It supports you before you even think about it, working together with the breath, the diaphragm, the pelvic floor and the deep muscles around the spine.

So we start where we all started: on the floor, with the breath and simple positions inspired by how babies first learn to stabilise and move. The first part warms up the deep core. Only then do we move on to the classic exercises: the dead bug, bird dog and planks, done released but deep.

Think of the old Taoist idea of **wu wei**, or effortless action: doing exactly what's needed, and nothing more.

## The rules that apply to every exercise

- **Don't brace.** Let the breath do the work first.
- **Position over range of motion.** Small and clean beats big and compensated.
- **Never force into a position that makes you compensate.**
- **Engage, but don't grip.**
- **A little shaking is normal.** Stop if pain goes above 2–3 out of 10.
- **Don't rush to the advanced versions.**

## The four stages of learning

You'll move from not noticing your bracing (unconsciously incompetent), to noticing it (consciously incompetent), to controlling it when you concentrate (consciously competent), and finally to your body supporting itself without you thinking about it (unconsciously competent). That last one is the goal.

## How much and how often (all advised)

- **Advised: one round a day,** if you have the time. Not every day is needed.
- **Advised reps: about 8–10,** or the number of breaths or hold time shown.
- A round takes about 20 minutes while you're learning, and 10–15 once you know it.
- If you also have the Hips programme, you can do both together, alternate them day by day, or split them across the week.

## What you'll need

A mat or towel, a wall, and a cushion. Optional for the harder versions: a yoga (exercise) ball.

This is education and movement coaching. If you have a medical condition, an injury, or you're pregnant or recently had a baby, check with your doctor or physiotherapist before you start.
